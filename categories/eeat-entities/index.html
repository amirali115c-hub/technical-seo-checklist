15 E-E-A-T Checks - Updated August 2026

E-E-A-T and Entity Checklist

Google introduced Experience into E-A-T in December 2022, turning the acronym into E-E-A-T. It is not a direct ranking factor, but the Search Quality Rater Guidelines are trained on it, and low-quality ratings precede visibility drops in core updates. This checklist builds the Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trust signals on your site and connects your brand into a single entity Google can recognize.

Trust Is an Entity Signal

An entity is a distinct, well-defined concept with properties and relationships. Your brand is an entity, your authors are entities, and Google's Knowledge Graph is a database of entities connected by facts. When your name, logo, and description are consistent everywhere and linked with schema, Google can attribute content to the right entity and reward the authority that coverage implies.

Author pages with credentials, a real about page, published dates, and updated content are the visible scaffolding of trust. YMYL topics, health, finance, and news, carry the highest bar because wrong information harms people. Google's rater guidelines demand demonstrated expertise there, not just well-written summaries.

The pattern that fails is anonymous content with no author, no dates, no credentials, and a generic about page. The pattern that wins is a named expert, a dated and updated article, citations, and consistent brand identity across the open web. This page is part of the complete technical SEO checklist.

Deep Dive: Every Check Explained

How to build credibility signals that survive core updates.

1 Create Detailed Author Pages With Credentials

Each author needs a dedicated page with a bio, qualifications, relevant experience, and links to their work. Anonymous content is the strongest negative E-E-A-T signal there is. The author page is the evidence file Google and your readers can inspect.

2 Add Person Schema With SameAs Links

Mark up every author with the Person schema and include sameAs links to their LinkedIn, Twitter, and other verified profiles. The sameAs property tells Google these identifiers all describe one person, which is how an author entity is assembled.

3 Add Organization Schema With SameAs Links

Mark up your company with Organization schema, including logo, founding date, and sameAs links to Wikipedia, Crunchbase, LinkedIn, and review platforms. Consistent identifiers across authoritative sources is what builds the Knowledge Graph entry for your brand.

4 Show a Clear Author Byline on Every Article

Every article needs a visible byline linking to the author page, not a hidden meta tag. The byline connects content to the author entity and lets readers judge the source. It is both a transparency signal and an entity attribution signal.

5 Build a Real About Page With Evidence

Your about page should state who runs the company, what qualifies the team, and where the operation is based. Evidence beats adjectives. Concrete facts give Google's raters something to verify instead of vague marketing claims.

6 Publish Dates and Keep Content Updated

Show publish and update dates on articles and keep them honest. For topics that change, updated content with a reviewed stamp signals maintenance. Stale dated content on fast-moving topics signals neglect, which rater guidelines penalize.

7 Match Author Expertise to Topic

Financial advice should come from a qualified finance professional, health content from someone with medical credentials. Mismatched expertise reads as content farm behavior. The author's demonstrated background must justify the topic, especially on YMYL subjects.

8 Standardize Name, Logo, and Description Everywhere

Use exactly one brand name, one logo, and one consistent description across your site, profiles, and citations. Fragmented identity confuses entity resolution. Google should see the same facts in the same order everywhere it looks.

9 Collect Authoritative Third-Party Mentions

Authoritativeness is conferred by others: press coverage, industry mentions, expert roundups, and citations from established sites. This is the hard work of digital PR. Unlinked brand mentions still build the entity, and linked ones pass authority.

10 Add Genuine Reviews and Testimonials

Authentic reviews on Google Business Profile, Trustpilot, and your site are trust signals. Never fabricate reviews. If you add review schema, it must reflect real user-generated ratings or you risk a manual action for deceptive structured data.

11 Publish Original Research and Data

Original surveys, studies, and datasets are the strongest Experience and Expertise signals because nobody else has them. They attract citations, earn links, and give Google a reason to treat your entity as a primary source rather than a summarizer.

12 Remove or Fix Low-Value Scaled Content

Google's March 2024 core update added scaled content abuse to its spam policies, targeting mass-produced content that adds little value regardless of how it is made. Audit for thin, unhelpful pages and delete or substantially improve them. Quality over volume.

13 Show Contact and Company Transparency

Publish a contact page with real contact methods, a physical address where applicable, and clear ownership. Opaque sites with no way to verify the operator are a negative trust signal. Transparency is the cheapest trust investment available.

14 Verify Schema and Fix Markup Errors

Run every page with schema through the Rich Results Test. Invalid or mismatched markup is ignored at best and can trigger manual actions at worst. Schema is the machine-readable side of entity building, so it must be correct.

15 Monitor the Knowledge Panel and Brand SERP

Search your brand and your authors quarterly. A knowledge panel, correct ownership claim, and clean brand SERP indicate a healthy entity. No panel yet is not a failure; it means the entity signals are still being assembled from the web.

Common E-E-A-T Mistakes

The credibility failures that core updates punish.

Anonymous content

No author, no byline, no credentials. Anonymous content is the single most damaging E-E-A-T gap on a site.

Fabricated experience

Claims of product testing or expertise without evidence. Reviewers and crawlers both catch the pattern eventually.

Missing or stale dates

Undated or silently updated content on time-sensitive topics reads as neglect. Show publish and update dates.

Inconsistent identity

Different names, logos, and descriptions across profiles fragment your entity and block knowledge panel assembly.

Scaled thin content

Mass-produced pages that add no value now trigger scaled content abuse policies regardless of how they are produced.

Fake reviews and ratings

Fabricated review schema or testimonials risk manual action for deceptive structured data. Only markup genuine ratings.

E-E-A-T Frequently Asked Questions

Direct answers on trust, entities, and Knowledge Graph.

What is E-E-A-T in SEO?

Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trust. The framework Google's quality raters use to judge content quality, with Experience added in 2022.

How does an entity help SEO?

A distinct, consistent concept Google can recognize. When your brand is one entity across the web, Google can attribute content, show richer results, and reward authoritative coverage.

What is sameAs structured data?

The sameAs property links a Person or Organization to authoritative profiles elsewhere, such as Wikipedia, Crunchbase, and LinkedIn, connecting identifiers into one entity.

Does E-E-A-T improve rankings?

Not a direct ranking factor, but rater guidelines are trained on it and low-quality ratings precede core update drops. Trust and authority protect rankings.

How do I get a Knowledge Panel?

You build the signals, Google compiles the panel. Consistent identity, accurate schema, and authoritative mentions across the web are the ingredients.

What counts as experience for E-E-A-T?

Direct, first-hand knowledge of the topic. For YMYL content, Google rewards lived experience and demonstrable expertise over paraphrased summaries.
