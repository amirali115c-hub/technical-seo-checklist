Technical SEO Checklist for Non-Technical Marketers

This is a technical SEO checklist for the non technical marketer. You do not need to be a developer to improve technical SEO. Here is what you can fix yourself, when to call for help, which free tools to use, and how to communicate with developers effectively.

You Can Do More Than You Think

Most technical SEO issues are detected with free tools, not code. As a marketer, you can identify problems, document them clearly, and in many cases fix them yourself through your CMS or site settings.

The misconception that technical SEO requires a developer stops many marketers from making simple, high-impact fixes. Checking index coverage, reviewing mobile usability, and finding broken links are all done with free tools that anyone can learn in minutes.

This guide separates what you can fix yourself from what requires a developer, with exact instructions for both scenarios.

Deep Dive: What You Can Fix Yourself

Step-by-step guides for the 10 things you can do without a developer.

1 Monitor Index Coverage Weekly

Open Google Search Console, go to Pages report. Check the total indexed vs not indexed count. Click each exclusion reason to understand why pages are not indexed. If important pages are not indexed, check if they have internal links and quality content.

2 Submit and Update XML Sitemaps

If you use Yoast, Rank Math, or another SEO plugin, your sitemap is auto-generated. Submit the sitemap URL to GSC under Sitemaps. After publishing new content, request indexing for the specific URL in GSC URL Inspection tool.

3 Fix Title Tags and Meta Descriptions

In your CMS (WordPress, Shopify, Webflow), edit each page's SEO title and meta description. Title: include the target keyword near the start, keep under 60 characters. Meta description: 2-3 sentences summarizing the page, include a call to action.

4 Run Page Speed Tests

Go to PageSpeed Insights, enter your URL. Review the suggestions in the "Opportunities" section. Screenshot the results and share with your developer. Common fixes: compress images, remove unused CSS, defer JavaScript.

5 Check Mobile Usability

Check GSC Mobile Usability report. Common issues: content wider than screen, clickable elements too close, text too small to read. Resize images, increase button sizes, and increase font size to 16px minimum in your theme settings.

6 Update Internal Links Between Posts

While editing a blog post in your CMS, add 2-3 links to other relevant posts on your site. Use descriptive anchor text. This distributes PageRank and helps Google understand content relationships.

7 Find and Remove Thin Content

Use Google Analytics to find pages with high bounce rate and low time on page. Check GSC for pages with zero impressions. Consolidate or remove thin content pages that add no value. Set up 301 redirects to related pages.

8 Review Search Performance and Queries

GSC Performance report shows which queries drive traffic, average position, click-through rate, and impressions. Identify pages ranking on page 2 (positions 10-20) and improve their content and internal links.

9 Set Up GSC Email Alerts

In GSC Settings, configure email notifications for: index coverage changes, crawl errors, security issues, and manual actions. This ensures you know about critical issues within hours, not weeks.

10 Run a Free Screaming Frog Crawl

Download Screaming Frog (free tier crawls 500 URLs). Enter your URL and start the crawl. Review the report for: 404 errors, missing title tags, missing meta descriptions, duplicate content. Share the report with your developer for fixes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions from non-technical marketers.

Can I do technical SEO without knowing how to code?

Yes. Many technical SEO tasks are done through Google Search Console, your CMS (WordPress, Shopify, Webflow), SEO plugins (Yoast, Rank Math), and free tools. You can fix meta tags, submit sitemaps, check page speed, and find broken links without code.

When should I call a developer for technical SEO?

Call a developer for: server-level redirects (htaccess, Nginx), caching configuration and CDN setup, security headers and HSTS, server-side schema markup, TTFB and server response optimization, and JavaScript rendering fixes.

What free tools can I use for technical SEO?

Google Search Console (indexing, CWV, errors), PageSpeed Insights (performance), Screaming Frog free tier (crawling up to 500 URLs), Rich Results Test (schema), Bing Webmaster Tools (IndexNow), and securityheaders.com (security audit). All free, all no-code.

How do I explain technical SEO to my boss?

Technical SEO is like the foundation of a building. Nobody sees it, but if it cracks, everything on top falls apart. It makes sure search engines can find, understand, and trust your website. Without it, content and backlinks cannot do their job.

Should I hire an agency or learn technical SEO myself?

Learn the basics yourself (this checklist covers everything). For complex issues like site migrations, JavaScript SEO, or enterprise-level infrastructure, hire an agency. The knowledge you gain will help you evaluate agency work and communicate effectively.
